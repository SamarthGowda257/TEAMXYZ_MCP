## How you receive a lab report
A finished report is sent to the mobile number and email address registered on your record. You can also collect a stamped printed copy from the diagnostic reception in Basement 1, or see it in the patient portal.

## Who a report can be sent to
Lab reports and biopsy findings are confidential medical records. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act and the medical council's code of ethics, we release them only to the verified patient. We cannot send a report to a third party or a company email address without the patient confirming it from their own registered mobile number.

## Getting a copy of your records
Copies of your medical records are given to you, or to a family member you have authorised in writing, on showing a photo identity document. Ask at the records room in Basement 1 or through the patient portal.

## Asking for a prescription refill
A refill is approved only by a doctor. For a regular long-term medicine we can raise a refill request with your consultant and tell you when it is approved. For antibiotics, sedatives or strong painkillers you need a fresh consultation, because the law does not allow those to be refilled over chat or the phone.

## Telemedicine rules for medicines
Under the national telemedicine guidelines, antibiotics, sleep medicines and psychiatric medicines cannot be prescribed or refilled over a voice or chat conversation without a proper video or in-person examination. If you are within your free seven-day follow-up window, use it to see your consultant and get a valid prescription.

## After your visit, how we check on you
After a procedure or a new medicine we check how you are getting on. We ask for a pain score out of ten, whether you have a fever and whether you feel better, the same or worse. What you tell us is recorded on your file for your doctor to see.

## When we alert a nurse after your visit
If your pain is seven or more out of ten, if you have a fever, or if you describe heavy bleeding, a badly swollen wound, sudden loss of vision or breathing difficulty, we raise an urgent alert on the nursing console and a triage nurse calls your registered number. If your condition is getting worse quickly, do not wait for the call; dial 108 or come to emergency at Gate 1.

## Mild symptoms after a procedure
Mild discomfort in the first two or three days after a procedure is common. Keep taking the medicines as prescribed and follow the instructions you were given on discharge. Because the first follow-up review within seven days carries no consultation fee, the simplest thing is to let us book that review with your consultant.

## Discharge
Discharge is usually completed before 12 noon, after the doctor's review. You are given a discharge summary, a medicine list and a follow-up date. If you choose to leave against medical advice, you will be asked to sign a form recording that.

## Valuables during a stay
Please do not bring jewellery or large amounts of cash into the hospital. We are not responsible for valuables left in rooms, though the nursing station can keep small items in a locker for you.
